# UK REITs — Grainger, Derwent, PHP, Big Yellow

As of 27 Aug 2026. Prices: last close 27 Aug 2026 (Yahoo Finance / LSE). Asset values: latest company reports (Grainger HY26 to 31 Mar 2026; Derwent and PHP H1 to 30 Jun 2026; Big Yellow FY to 31 Mar 2026).

This is a value screen, not a buy list. For a property company, **EPRA NTA / adjusted NAV is the asset floor, not intrinsic value**. Intrinsic value is the present value of owner earnings (EPRA earnings / adjusted earnings), plus any development surplus that has not yet been booked, minus leverage risk. A wide discount to NTA is necessary, not sufficient: NTA itself can fall when gilt yields rise, and UK REITs have sat at 30–45% discounts for years without closing.

**None of the four is a strong buy.** Big Yellow is the closest to a value buy (quality + 35% NAV discount + 15% LTV). Grainger has the widest discount and an activist, but earnings do not yet cover the dividend and LTV is 40%. Derwent is cheap versus book with a real chance of being a London-office value trap. PHP is an income vehicle trading near par with 57% LTV — not a value purchase.

---

## How to read these numbers

- **Price / NTA** uses last close vs last reported EPRA NTA (or Big Yellow’s adjusted NAV, which is EPRA NTA with 2.75% purchaser’s costs). A 0.60x ratio means the market prices the equity at 40% below stated net assets.
- **Earnings yield** uses last reported or guided EPRA / adjusted EPS ÷ price. That is the cash-earnings return if NTA never changes.
- **Payout** is dividend ÷ EPRA (or adjusted) earnings, not IFRS profit. IFRS profit for REITs is dominated by valuation swings and is the wrong cover test.
- **VC takeovers** of listed UK REITs are not a real path. REIT rules, size, and the need for cheap long-term debt make the buyers PE / infra / other REITs (PHP bought Assura in 2025; Prologis bid for SEGRO in 2026). Hedge-fund activism is live: Saba Capital has shifted from investment trusts into UK property names trading at wide NAV discounts.

## 1. Grainger plc — GRI — residential / build-to-rent

UK’s only listed, scaled pure-play BTR landlord. Converted to REIT 8 Sep 2025. Occupancy 95.9%, like-for-like rental growth +3.1% in HY26. Committed pipeline is on fixed-price contracts; management targets £60m EPRA earnings (8.1p) in FY26 and £72m (9.7p) by FY29.

### Risks (beyond generic tenant / recession risk)

- **Regulation.** Rent controls were not imposed in the last parliamentary round, but Section 21 abolition, EPC minimums, and tenant-rights bills can cap rent and force non-revenue capex. Residential is the most politicised UK property sector.
- **Leverage and refinancing.** Group LTV 40.2% (FY25: 38.4%), net debt £1.52bn, average cost of debt 3.2%. Management wants LTV ~30% and net debt/EBITDA ~8x by FY29 via disposals. Until then, rolling cheap fixed debt onto higher coupons is the earnings drag.
- **Development / lease-up.** Occupancy fell from 98.0% (FY25) to 95.9% as new buildings lease up. A UK recession that hits household formation slows that lease-up.
- **NTA already falling.** EPRA NTA 290p vs 298p at FY25 (−3%) on ~25bp outward yield shift. The 40% discount is to a *declining* book.
- **Dividend not yet covered by EPRA earnings.** FY25 DPS 8.31p vs EPRA EPS 7.3p (114%). Post-REIT policy is a minimum 80% of EPRA earnings, topped up from regulated-tenancy sales in FY26–27, fully covered by EPRA from FY28. That is a planned path, not a fortress payout.

### Rewards

- Structural UK housing shortage; BTR demand has held occupancy near 97.5% through recent cycles (company).
- Locked-in earnings from the committed pipeline without needing new equity if the disposals plan works.
- REIT conversion removes the old tax drag and forces a higher payout from FY28.
- **Saba Capital 6.31%** (disclosed 29 Jun 2026). Same activist that tried to force a Workspace wind-down and lost the Jul 2026 AGM. Optionality: buybacks, faster disposals, or a managed wind-down. Not a bid. QuotedData (Jul 2026) flagged Grainger as a possible Saba wind-down candidate; treat that as a scenario, not a base case.

### Price vs intrinsic value

- **Fact:** 173.1p vs 290p NTA = 0.60x. FY26 guided EPRA 8.1p → earnings yield **4.7%** at this price. That is gilt-like income on a levered residential book.
- **Estimate — base IV ~220–250p.** Haircut NTA 15–25% for residential liquidity, regulation, and further yield drift; add a little for the committed pipeline. At 173p that is a **20–30% margin of safety vs base**, ~40% vs unhaircut NTA.
- **Bear:** further 50bp yield expansion plus slower lease-up → NTA ~230–250p and the discount stays. **Bull:** NTA stabilises, LTV falls, Saba forces capital return → price toward 250–290p.

### Dividend

Sustainable *if* EPRA hits £60m in FY26 and the regs-sales top-up continues through FY27. Not conservative today. Yield ~4.8% is not the reason to own it; the discount to NTA is.

### Value verdict

**Not a strong buy.** Widest discount of the four and real activist optionality, but you are buying a 40% LTV developer whose cash earnings yield is ~5% and whose dividend is still topped up. A patient value account can nibble. It is not a Buffett-style great business at a stupid price.

## 2. Derwent London — DLN — central London offices

Design-led West End / City offices. H1 2026: new lettings £22.1m at +5.1% vs ERV; EPRA vacancy 4.4% (would be 3.5% if a vacant sale completes); WAULT to break 6.0 years (6.8 topped-up). EPRA LTV 28.8%, net debt £1.38bn, interest cover 3.0x. £50m buyback underway; £18.1m done in H1 at an average **1,833p** (cheaper than today’s 2,050p).

### Risks

- **Structural office demand.** Hybrid work shrinks footprint even when “Grade A” still lets. Derwent’s product is the right end of the barbell; the barbell can still move.
- **Brown-discount / capex.** Keeping buildings BREEAM Outstanding / NABERS 5-star is a continuous capital call. Stranded older stock is the sector’s real tenant-loss risk, not a sudden mass default.
- **Cap rates.** London offices reprice when long gilt yields rise. H1 NTA fell 2.1% to 3,157p on 6bp outward yield shift **plus** a £45.8m provision on Old Street Quarter EC1 (to be acquired late 2027). More provisions are possible before completion.
- **Earnings going the wrong way.** EPRA EPS 98.4p in 2025 (−7.6%), 48.7p in H1 2026 (−6.7%). 2026 guidance upgraded only to “0% to −3%.” Dividend is growing while earnings shrink — cover is still 1.2x, but the gap is closing.
- **Lease expiry.** Portfolio WAULT 6.0 years to break. That is the “contracts ending” risk, concentrated in a few HQ floors rather than thousands of flats.

### Rewards

- Best-in-class London product still leasing above ERV; Databricks pre-let at Network W1 is evidence that flight-to-quality is real.
- Low leverage vs the sector; buybacks at a 40%+ discount to NTA are accretive if NTA is not a fantasy.
- 18 consecutive years of dividend growth through FY25; H1 2026 DPS +2% to 26.0p, covered by H1 EPRA EPS 48.7p.

### Price vs intrinsic value

- **Fact:** 2,050p vs 3,157p NTA = 0.65x. FY25 EPRA 98.4p → earnings yield **4.8%**. Consensus (S&P Global, 14 analysts, late Aug 2026) is **Hold**, average target 2,046p — the Street does not expect the discount to close in 12 months.
- **Estimate — base IV ~2,400–2,800p.** Haircut NTA 10–25% for office cap-rate and vacancy risk. At 2,050p that is a thin-to-moderate MOS if you believe Grade A holds; **little MOS in the bear** (NTA −15–20% from here → ~2,500–2,700p NTA, price can sit at 1,700–1,900p).
- **Bull:** yields stable, Old Street not a sinkhole, buybacks continue → price toward 2,600–3,000p. Saba’s 0.10% stake is not a campaign.

### Dividend

FY25 81.5p / 98.4p = **83% payout, 1.2x cover.** Policy is progressive but “well covered.” Sustainable at current cover; **not sustainable if EPRA keeps falling 7% a year.** Yield ~4.0% is the weakest of the four.

### Value verdict

**Not a strong buy.** Classic value-trap shape: cheap vs a book the market does not trust, earnings declining, Street targets at the current price. Own it only if you have a specific view that West End Grade A is through the worst of the yield shift. Low LTV and buybacks are the margin of safety, not a catalyst.

## 3. Primary Health Properties — PHP — GP surgeries / healthcare

UK’s largest listed healthcare REIT after the **2025 combination with Assura**. Portfolio £6.0bn, 99% occupancy, WAULT 10.4 years, **76% of rent government-backed** (NHS / HSE), 13% private hospitals. Adjusted EPS H1 2026 +9% to 3.8p. 92% of identified Assura cost synergies delivered; EPRA cost ratio 8.7%.

### Risks

- **Monopsony.** One ultimate payor. NHS funding, GP contract reform, or a policy shift away from private capital for primary care caps growth. This is not a tenant-default risk; it is a rent-review-and-new-build risk.
- **Leverage after Assura.** **LTV 57%** vs a 40–50% target and a 65% bank covenant. Net debt drawn £3.4bn. Interest cover 2.57x (covenant 1.1x). Management plans JVs and disposals to cut LTV toward ~53% by year-end 2026. Until LTV is back inside 50%, this is the most levered name on the list. A gilt-driven valuation dip would tighten the covenant headroom fast.
- **Bond-proxy.** Income is long and sovereign-like, so the share trades off gilt yields. When gilts rise, PHP falls until the 7%+ yield re-clears the market. That is why it sits near NTA while the others sit at 35–40% discounts — the market already believes the book.
- **Lease roll.** 20% of income is holding over or expires within three years (£69m). PHP says renewal is “extremely unlikely” to fail given no speculative supply. Still the contract-expiry risk you flagged; it is concentrated, not diversified like storage.
- **Inflation mismatch.** Build cost for clinics has outrun the rent the NHS will underwrite, which stalls the development engine that is supposed to grow earnings after the merger synergies.

### Rewards

- 30 consecutive years of dividend growth; 2026 run-rate 7.3p (+2.8%), **103% covered** by adjusted earnings, policy is fully covered progressive.
- Occupancy 99%, WAULT 10.4 years, 76% government-backed — closest thing in UK listed property to a long gilt with a bit of rental growth.
- Scale after Assura: liquidity, lower cost of capital, £8m+ cost synergies. PHP is the consolidator, not the prey.

### Price vs intrinsic value

- **Fact:** 96p vs 104p adjusted NTA (99p IFRS NTA) = **0.92x**. Almost no asset discount. Earnings yield on ~7.6p full-year adjusted run-rate ≈ **7.9%**.
- **Estimate — base IV ~100–110p.** You are paying roughly fair value for a levered, government-backed income stream. **Bear:** gilts up, LTV stuck, NTA 90–95p and a freeze (not a cut) in the dividend. **Bull:** LTV to 45%, yield compression, 110–120p.
- Takeover: **low.** PHP just did the sector’s big deal. VC/PE would have to pay near NTA for NHS-leased bricks — no bargain.

### Dividend

Highest yield (~7.6%) and longest growth streak. Cover is **thin (103%)**, not fat. Sustainable if (a) Assura earnings stick, (b) LTV falls so interest cost does not eat the cover, (c) NHS rents keep reviewing up ~3%. A 57% LTV book with 103% cover is **not** a fortress payout. Do not underwrite 30 more years of increases from here.

### Value verdict

**Not a buy for value; a possible hold for income.** Near-par to NTA, high LTV, thin cover. The 7.6% yield is the whole thesis. A value investor who needs a margin of safety on assets walks past this.

## 4. Big Yellow Group — BYG — self-storage

UK brand leader, 111 stores, 6.7m sq ft MLA, 99% freehold / long-leasehold by value, ~75% of revenue in London and commuter towns. Store EBITDA margin **70.5%**. FY26: revenue +2% to £209.1m, adjusted PBT +2% to £117.5m, adjusted EPS 59.0p, DPS 47.2p. LTV **15%**, net debt £477m.

### Risks

- **Occupancy, not tenants.** Storage is monthly, not 10-year leases. Closing occupancy 74.2% (headline, diluted by four new stores) vs 78.7% prior year; like-for-like 77.0% (−1.7ppt). Management said the LFL gap had narrowed to 0.6ppt after year-end. 40% of move-ins are house moves. A frozen housing market is the demand shock.
- **Low barriers at the margin.** Converting an industrial shed into storage is easier than building a West End office. Moat is brand, main-road sites, and density in London — real, but not a patent.
- **Business rates.** Large footprints. Company flagged an 8.5% rates rise in 2027 inside 2–3% LFL opex inflation.
- **NAV methodology.** Adjusted NAV 1,370.4p uses 2.75% purchaser’s costs (corporate-sale assumption), 60.8p/share above a standard Red Book. The published NAV is the optimistic one.

### Rewards

- Operating leverage: once a store is built, incremental occupancy is high-margin. 70.5% store EBITDA is a real moat metric.
- **Fortress balance sheet.** 15% LTV is in a different league from PHP’s 57% and Grainger’s 40%.
- Dividend policy: **minimum 80% of adjusted EPS**; FY26 paid exactly that (47.2 / 59.0). Covered by cash from operations £111.5m. FY26 DPS +2%.
- The **previous read that Big Yellow “often trades at a premium to NAV” is outdated.** At 887p vs 1,370p it is **0.65x**. Historically the operating platform earned a premium; the current discount is the opportunity.

### Price vs intrinsic value

- **Fact:** 887p vs 1,370.4p adj. NAV = **0.65x (−35%)**. Adjusted EPS 59.0p → earnings yield **6.7%**, plus a 5.3% dividend. Best earnings yield of the four on the strongest balance sheet.
- **Estimate — base IV ~1,100–1,300p.** Haircut NAV ~10–20% for occupancy risk and the generous purchaser’s-cost assumption. At 887p that is a **20–35% MOS vs base**. **Bear:** occupancy stays ~75%, housing stays frozen, NAV drifts to ~1,150p, price 700–800p. **Bull:** occupancy back above 80%, housing thaws, market re-rates toward 1.0x NAV → 1,200–1,400p.
- Takeover: storage has been a PE hunting ground (Blackstone looked at the sector in prior years). No live bid in the 2026 tape. A 35% discount to a 15% LTV freehold portfolio is the sort of gap that *can* attract a bid; do not underwrite one.

### Dividend

**Best payout design of the four:** 80% of adjusted earnings, fully cash-covered, low leverage. Sustainable as long as occupancy does not collapse. A 5.3% yield that can grow with store openings is more durable than PHP’s 7.6% on 57% LTV.

### Value verdict

**Buy, not a strong buy.** Closest to the value formula: good business (brand, margins, freehold, 15% LTV) at a 35% discount to NAV and a 6.7% earnings yield. Occupancy is the open question; until LFL occupancy is clearly rising again, this is a buy with a job to do, not a scream.

## Takeovers, activists, VC

| Path | Realistic? | Where it matters |
|---|---|---|
| VC buyout | No | Wrong capital, REIT rules, ticket size |
| PE / infra take-private | Sometimes | Big Yellow (storage platforms); theoretically Grainger BTR |
| REIT-on-REIT M&A | Yes, just happened | PHP already bought Assura (2025). PHP is not a target. |
| Activist (Saba) | Live | **Grainger 6.31%**. Derwent 0.10% is noise. Saba lost the Workspace AGM (Jul 2026); UK institutions may not back a wind-down. |
| Strategic (e.g. Prologis/SEGRO pattern) | Possible in logistics, not these four | Shows the sector *can* re-rate on a bid; not a thesis for GRI/DLN/PHP/BYG |

Do not pay for a bid. Treat Saba at Grainger and PE interest in storage as free (low-probability) upside.

## Ranking for a value account

1. **Big Yellow** — quality + discount + low debt. Buy on weakness if occupancy stabilises.
2. **Grainger** — cheapest vs book, activist kicker, unfinished earnings/leverage story. Small position only.
3. **Derwent** — cheap vs book, possible trap. Only with a London Grade-A view.
4. **PHP** — skip for value; income investors can own it after LTV is back below 50%.

**Strong buy: none.**
